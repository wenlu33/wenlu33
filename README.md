# Wen Lu

I am an Economics PhD candidate at Georgia State University. My research studies how prices, information, and policy affect behavior. I build laboratory experiments in Python and oTree, work with large public survey datasets, and explain economic findings through papers, reports, and teaching.

## Selected work

### Pricing and recommendations

I developed a congestion experiment comparing a fixed charge with private recommendations. The study involved 170 participants in six sessions and generated 3,400 repeated decisions.

[Experiment code](https://github.com/wenlu33/ioi) · [Research website](https://www.wenlu.site)

### Survey data and economic policy

My adolescent-health research combines survey responses with state policy and unemployment data. The cleaned pooled sample contains 400,742 responses across 43 sites in 2015, 2017, and 2019. The original analysis uses Stata. A new Python/SQL companion documents source coding, sample construction, policy-data linkage, and validation, with a runnable synthetic example.

[Survey workflow](https://github.com/wenlu33/survey-microdata-workflow) · [Case study](https://github.com/wenlu33/survey-microdata-workflow/blob/main/reports/case_study.md)

### Research communication

At USC, I coauthored *Inequality in the US: The Lost American Dream*, an accessible technical report on income, wealth, health, and education. I have also taught microeconomics and environmental economics to 145 undergraduates at Georgia State.
